================================================================================
VULNERABILITY REPORT: Subdomain Takeover via Dangling Mailgun CNAME
================================================================================

Program:        Figma Bug Bounty (HackerOne)
Target:         *.figma.com (in-scope)
Affected Asset: email.recruiting.figma.com
Severity:       HIGH
Type:           Subdomain Takeover
CWE:            CWE-284 (Improper Access Control)
                CWE-350 (Reliance on Reverse DNS Resolution for a Security-Critical Action)

================================================================================
SUMMARY
================================================================================

The subdomain email.recruiting.figma.com has a CNAME record pointing to
mailgun.org with full email infrastructure DNS records (MX, SPF, TXT)
configured, but no active domain is registered in any Mailgun account.
This allows an attacker to register the domain in their own Mailgun account,
pass DNS verification automatically (since the records already exist), and
gain full control over email routing for this subdomain.

The attacker can then:
- Intercept all inbound emails to *@email.recruiting.figma.com
- Send outbound emails from @email.recruiting.figma.com that pass SPF checks
- Conduct targeted phishing against job candidates impersonating Figma recruiting

================================================================================

STEPS TO REPRODUCE
================================================================================

Step 1: Verify the subdomain returns 404 (no active Mailgun domain)

    $ curl -sI https://email.recruiting.figma.com
    HTTP/1.1 404 Not Found
    Content-Type: text/plain; charset=utf-8

    $ curl -s https://email.recruiting.figma.com
    404 page not found

Step 2: Verify CNAME points to mailgun.org

    $ dig CNAME email.recruiting.figma.com +short
    mailgun.org.

Step 3: Verify MX records point to Mailgun

    $ dig MX email.recruiting.figma.com +short
    10 mxa.mailgun.org.
    10 mxb.mailgun.org.

Step 4: Verify SPF record authorizes Mailgun

    $ dig TXT email.recruiting.figma.com +short
    "v=spf1 include:_spf.mailgun.org include:_spf.eu.mailgun.org -all"

Step 5: Register domain in attacker's Mailgun account

    - Go to https://app.mailgun.com/
    - Create free account (or use existing)
    - Navigate to Sending > Domains > Add New Domain
    - Enter: email.recruiting.figma.com
    - Mailgun checks DNS for required records (CNAME, MX, SPF)
    - All records already exist -> domain is VERIFIED automatically
    - Attacker now controls email for this subdomain

================================================================================
IMPACT
================================================================================

1. EMAIL INTERCEPTION (Inbound):
   - Attacker creates Mailgun routes to capture all emails sent to
     *@email.recruiting.figma.com
   - Intercepts recruiting communications: candidate responses, interview
     scheduling, offer letter confirmations, internal links/tokens
   - Potential exposure of candidate PII (names, emails, phone numbers,
     resume data, compensation discussions)

2. EMAIL SPOOFING (Outbound):
   - Attacker sends emails FROM @email.recruiting.figma.com
   - Emails PASS SPF validation because Mailgun is explicitly authorized
     in the SPF record (include:_spf.mailgun.org)
   - Phishing scenarios:
     * Fake job offers with malicious document attachments
     * "Complete your background check" links to credential harvesting pages
     * "Update your banking details for direct deposit" social engineering
     * Internal Figma employee targeting ("new recruiting tool, please login")

3. BRAND/REPUTATION DAMAGE:
   - Attacker can send spam/malware from a legitimate Figma subdomain
   - Recipients see emails from @email.recruiting.figma.com in their inbox
   - Damages Figma's email reputation and deliverability

4. POTENTIAL COOKIE SCOPE ABUSE:
   - If the attacker can serve web content on email.recruiting.figma.com
     (the 404 endpoint suggests a web server is reachable), they may be
     able to set cookies scoped to .figma.com
   - This could enable session fixation or other cookie-based attacks
     against www.figma.com users

================================================================================
ATTACK SCENARIO
================================================================================

Realistic attack scenario targeting Figma job candidates:

1. Attacker claims email.recruiting.figma.com on their Mailgun account
2. Attacker sets up inbound route: match_recipient(".*@email.recruiting.figma.com")
   -> forward to attacker@evil.com
3. Attacker crafts phishing email:

   From: careers@email.recruiting.figma.com
   To: candidate@gmail.com
   Subject: Action Required: Figma Offer Letter - Please Review & Sign

   Hi [Candidate Name],

   Congratulations! We're excited to extend an offer for the Senior
   Product Designer role at Figma.

   Please review and sign your offer letter using our secure portal:
   https://figma-careers-portal.evil.com/offer/sign

   This link expires in 48 hours. If you have questions, reply to this
   email directly.

   Best,
   Figma Recruiting Team

4. Email passes SPF checks -> lands in inbox (not spam)
5. Candidate clicks link, enters personal information on fake portal
6. Attacker harvests SSN, banking details, identity documents

================================================================================
SEVERITY JUSTIFICATION
================================================================================

HIGH severity based on:

- No authentication required (free Mailgun account)
- Automated verification (DNS records pre-exist)
- Direct impact on real users (job candidates, Figma employees)
- Email interception = confidentiality breach
- Email spoofing = integrity breach (trusted sender impersonation)
- SPF-passing phishing significantly increases success rate
- Recruiting context provides high-value social engineering pretexts
- Multiple HackerOne precedents confirm this severity level:
  * Bitwarden #272357 - Mailgun misconfiguration (similar pattern)
  * Mail.ru #819309 - Mailgun subdomain takeover
  * Deriv.com #2270082 - Mailgun subdomain takeover (May 2024)

================================================================================
RECOMMENDED FIX
================================================================================

Option A: Remove dangling DNS records (preferred if email is no longer needed)

    Delete the following records from Figma's DNS zone:
    - email.recruiting.figma.com CNAME mailgun.org
    - All associated TXT records (SPF, verification tokens)

Option B: Reclaim domain in Figma's Mailgun account

    If Figma still uses Mailgun for recruiting emails:
    - Log into Figma's Mailgun account
    - Re-add email.recruiting.figma.com as an active domain
    - Configure proper routing rules
    - This prevents any other account from claiming it

Option C: Migrate to different email provider

    - Update CNAME to point to new provider
    - Update MX records accordingly
    - Update SPF record to authorize new provider
    - Remove old Mailgun verification TXT records
